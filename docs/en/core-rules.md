# Core Rules

In **Truchements**, the player characters are **Agents** bound to **Powers** that transcend Humanity.

A Power acts in the world through its Agents. An Agent is an Agent because they are bound to a Power. The exact nature of that bond depends on the Setting: service, lineage, incarnation, pact, creation, debt, or any other relationship established by the game world.

Settings give these elements a concrete form. They define the Powers that exist, the Agents that can be played, their relationship with Humanity, and the situations that set them in motion.

:::note[Some Possible Powers]

In **Choirs & Legions**, a Power might be an Archangel or a Demon Prince. In **Ancient & New Gods**, it might be a Norse deity or a forgotten god finding a new place in the modern world. **Blood & Night** may feature Vampires, bloodlines, or other Powers of the night. **Signals** may involve entities whose very nature is difficult to interpret.

These examples belong to their Settings. They do not form a shared cosmology for all of Truchements.

:::

## Playing

**Truchements** uses a traditional tabletop role-playing structure.

One person is the **Game Master (GM)**. They present the world, play its inhabitants and forces, describe their reactions, and establish the important consequences of choices.

The other participants are the **players**. Each usually plays an Agent and decides what they attempt, what they say, and how they act.

Conversation is enough whenever the fiction already makes the outcome clear. Resolution rules come into play when an important outcome remains genuinely uncertain.

## Resolving a Situation

A resolution always begins with the fiction.

The player first states their **Intention**: what they are actually trying to achieve.

> *I want to stop the priest from completing the ritual.*

The table then defines the **Focus**: the exact question the resolution must answer.

> *Is the ritual interrupted before the entity responds?*

The Focus places two possible outcomes in the balance. It does not describe everything that may happen; it only states what the dice need to decide.

The **Zoom** sets the scale of that question. A resolution can concern a single act, an entire sequence, or something much broader. The wider the Zoom, the more distinct elements of the fiction may become relevant.

### What is Possible

Before looking for Bets, the fiction determines **what the acting character can actually do**.

An ordinary Human cannot simply decide to fly. An Angel with wings can spread them and fly. If nothing makes that flight uncertain, no roll is needed: the Angel flies.

Likewise, a Power may grant its Agents possibilities that Humans — or Agents of other Powers — simply do not have.

These possibilities are written into the **Agent's portrait** as keywords: nature, abilities, gifts, bonds, states, or other elements that define them.

A keyword does not automatically grant a die. It first establishes what is true and what is possible in the fiction.

### Bets: What Matters Here

Once the action is possible, the table looks at **what genuinely matters to the Focus, at this Zoom**.

A relevant element becomes a **Bet**.

It may come from the character's portrait, their Power, relationships, past, the situation, environment, an injury, an object, preparation, or any other relevant element.

The same keyword may simply make an action possible in one scene, then become a Bet in another if the Focus gives it real weight.

Each Bet favors one of the two outcomes.

The same reason only counts once. *Exceptional warrior*, *trained since childhood*, and *veteran of a hundred battles* do not automatically form three Bets if, for this resolution, all three simply mean the same thing.

When nobody immediately sees anything else that matters without repeating what is already in play, resolve.

:::note[Example — Choirs & Legions]

An Agent wants to stop a group of occultists from opening a seal.

In the Agent's favor, the table might retain *Knows forbidden rites* and *Bears the mark of their Archangel*. In favor of the ritual, it might retain *The circle is almost complete*, *Three occultists maintain the chant*, and *The site has been prepared for weeks*.

These elements are the Bets of the resolution.

:::

An outcome may have **zero Bets**: it then starts with zero dice or zero Successes before any special rule is applied. If neither side has any Bet, however, there is probably nothing to resolve. The table returns to the fiction and asks what actually makes the situation uncertain.

## The Realization Scale

Each Bet normally contributes **one D6**.

How those dice become Successes depends on the **nature of what is acting**.

| Nature | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| **Neutral** | Each even result produces 1 Success. |
| **Humanity** | Each even result produces 1 Success. Each 6 from the initial roll may reroll one odd result. |
| **Agent** | Each even result produces 1 Success and may reroll one odd result. A 6 with no odd result left to reroll adds a new die. |
| **Power** | No roll: each Bet directly produces 1 Success. |

This scale is common to Truchements. A Setting may then specify particular features of its Powers and Agents.

### Neutral

**Neutral** resolution applies to matter, the environment, and forces that act neither as Humans, Agents, nor Powers.

Roll one D6 per Bet.

Each **even** result produces one Success. Each odd result produces none.

> 4 Bets → 4 dice → **2, 3, 4, 5** → **2 Successes**.

### Humanity

Humans remain close to Neutral resolution, but a **6** sometimes lets them recover from an unfavorable result.

Roll one D6 per Bet.

Each **even** result produces one Success. For each **6 obtained on the initial roll**, the player may reroll one odd result.

This reroll never creates a new die. It is not cumulative: if the rerolled die itself shows a 6, that 6 counts as a Success but does not grant another reroll.

> **2, 3, 5, 6** first gives 2 Successes. The 6 allows the player to reroll the 3 or the 5. If that reroll gives a 6, the total rises to 3 Successes, but the chain stops there.

### Agent

Agents have a much stronger hold over uncertainty.

Roll one D6 per Bet.

Each **even** result produces one Success **and may reroll one odd result**.

New results are resolved in the same way: a new even result may therefore reroll another odd result still in play.

If a **6** acts when there is no odd result left to reroll, it adds **one new die**. That new die is then resolved normally.

Cascades are therefore possible.

> **2, 3, 5, 6** gives 2 Successes. The 2 and the 6 may reroll the two odd results. If one of those rerolls produces another 6 when no odd result remains, that 6 adds a new die.

Some Settings may distinguish several degrees among Agents when their hierarchies or differences in power justify it. For now, the common rule above remains the reference.

### Power

A Power does not roll dice for its own Bets.

**Each Bet directly produces one Success.**

> 4 Bets → **4 Successes**.

A Power is therefore perfectly reliable at an equal number of Bets. An Agent may nevertheless, through rerolls and cascades, exceptionally produce more Successes than their initial number of Bets.

## Different Natures in the Same Resolution

Actors of different natures may act together.

Each resolves their own Bets according to their nature, then the Successes favoring the same outcome are added together before the final comparison.

A Human may therefore act with an Agent, several Agents may serve different Powers, and a Power may itself intervene in a resolution without requiring everyone to use the same rule.

## Comparing Successes

Once both sides have been resolved, compare their Successes.

If one side has more, **its outcome prevails in the fiction**.

On a tie, neither outcome clearly prevails: the situation remains in a **status quo**, or the actors may push the confrontation further if the fiction allows it.

A result is **dominated** when the difference is strictly greater than 1 Success and the winning side has strictly more than twice as many Successes as the other. Victory or Defeat may then be amplified: **Yes, and...** for an Exploit, **No, and...** for a Fiasco, when that fits the fiction.

Thus **2–0** and **3–1** are dominated; **4–2** is not.

## Interpreting the Result

The Bets return to the fiction after the roll.

They already tell the table what mattered in the situation and therefore provide the material for the result. An injury may worsen, a relationship may save a character, preparation may finally pay off, a place may become dangerous, or a manifestation of a Power may leave a mark.

The result does not replace the fiction: it lets the table decide in which direction it continues.

## Developing an Agent

An Agent's portrait changes with what happens to them.

A lasting consequence may **add, modify, or remove a keyword**. The Agent may gain a new gift, lose an ability, transform a bond, carry an injury, change status, or become something else.

These changes directly alter what becomes possible for them and what may become a Bet in future situations.

## Sixes and Settings

The **6** already has a role in Humanity and Agent resolutions.

Some Settings may also attach an effect to a 6 that is specific to a Power, a family of Powers, or the bond between a Power and its Agents.

:::note[Example]

In one Setting, a 6 might make something of the Power visible through its Agent: a sign, a presence, a transformation, or a particular consequence.

The exact form of these manifestations belongs to each Setting.

:::
